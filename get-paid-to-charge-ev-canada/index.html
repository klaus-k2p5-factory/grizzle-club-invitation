Why providers can pay

Private reward programs—not Government of Canada payments.

Canada's Clean Fuel Regulations establish a credit market. Federal guidance says charging network operators can create credits for residential EV charging and that revenues associated with residential and public charging credits must be reinvested in charging infrastructure, supporting electricity infrastructure or financial incentives for consumers. [8]

The providers set the consumer offers. Their applications, compatible hardware, rates, payout methods, data practices and termination rules are private program terms—not a government rebate, approval or guaranteed entitlement.

Connected charging data is operational

Eligible sessions must reach the provider's systems. Missed or disconnected sessions may not earn rewards, and current software or firmware can be required.

Credit rights can be exclusive

ChargeLab's terms require the exclusive right to claim carbon credits from enrolled residential charging. Other providers also assign environmental or incentive rights under their own terms.

One charger, one clear claimant

Do not enrol the same charger in conflicting carbon-credit programs. Confirm how to leave one program before connecting the charger to another.

Common questions

Three answers before you connect a charger.

Can Canadians get paid for charging an EV at home?

Some private programs reward eligible connected residential charging. Hardware, property, data, rates, payout and exit requirements differ.

Which program pays the most?

A single rate is not an apples-to-apples ranking. Compare starting hardware, ownership, deposit, installation, eligibility, payout method, threshold, future effective dates and rate-change rights.

Can one charger join several reward programs?

Do not enrol the same charger in conflicting programs. Read each provider's current credit-rights and exit terms before connecting another operator.
